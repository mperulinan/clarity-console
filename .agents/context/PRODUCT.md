# Product

## Register

product

## Users

The primary user is the developer — reviewing their own crypto portfolio occasionally at a desk, likely in the evening or low-ambient-light conditions. A secondary audience is technical interviewers seeing the app during a portfolio demo, on a Zoom call or shared screen. A tertiary audience may be other individual investors if the tool is ever opened to others. All three groups share a need for clarity and trust in the numbers; the interview audience adds the requirement that the interface demonstrate craft and intentionality.

## Product Purpose

A personal crypto portfolio tracker with tax computation. Tracks holdings, cost basis, realized and unrealized P&L, and generates wash-sale-aware tax reports by currency. Success looks like: numbers are immediately readable, the state of the portfolio is graspable in under five seconds, and a recruiter can tell the developer cares about UX quality without the interface announcing itself.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, serious. Not austere — there is warmth and craft in the details — but never decorative for its own sake. The emotional register is confident understatement: a tool that knows what it is.

## Anti-references

**Generic AI UI slop**: glowing purple/indigo/cyan neon gradients as default "futuristic energy," heavy glassmorphism with strong uniform backdrop blur and frosted translucent cards everywhere, floating holographic panels and orbs, neural network particles and sparkles.

**Overused dashboard clichés**: big pulsing glowing metric numbers with dramatic bloom and animated rings, rainbow/chrome gradient text on headings, sterile perfectly symmetrical layouts with excessive depth-of-field blur.

**Cheap cyber-futuristic tropes**: lone hoodie figure and massive holographic screens, brain-circuit overlays, heavy volumetric god rays and lens flares, plastic-smooth idealized rendering with no texture.

**Dribbble-bait generic SaaS**: overly clean uniform card grids, high-contrast cinematic lighting from multiple angles, injection-molded surfaces, every screen looking like a product launch mockup.

## Design Principles

1. **Precision over decoration.** Every visual element earns its place by communicating something about the data or the hierarchy. Ornament that carries no information is removed.
2. **Confident restraint.** The design knows what it is. Understatement implies mastery; the interface does not congratulate itself for existing.
3. **Data as the primary material.** Typography, spacing, and color exist to make financial data legible and trustworthy. Aesthetics serve readability, not the reverse.
4. **Cinematic without theatrics.** Portfolio demo quality means a recruiter thinks "this person understands craft" — not "this person watched too many sci-fi UI reels." Sophistication comes from spacing, weight, and motion timing.
5. **Hierarchy through control.** No particle effects, no pulsing rings, no gradient text. Visual interest is earned through layout asymmetry, weight contrast, and purposeful negative space.
6. **Delight through motion, not spectacle.** Micro-animations, subtle interactive flair, and shader-like effects are welcome when they enhance usability or reward attention. The bar is: does this make the interface feel more alive and responsive, or does it just perform? Transitions must feel intentional, not compensatory.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA as a baseline. Financial data must pass 4.5:1 contrast for text. Gain/loss colors must not rely solely on hue — pair with sign prefix, icons, or weight. Reduced motion preference respected via `prefers-reduced-motion`.
